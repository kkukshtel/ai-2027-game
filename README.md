# AGI Race: Brinkmanship - Rules Primer

## Game Overview

Welcome to the cutting edge of the 21st century. In *AGI Race: Brinkmanship*, you take control of either the **USA** or **China**, the two superpowers locked in a high-stakes competition to develop and control Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and potentially Artificial Superintelligence (ASI). This card-driven strategy game simulates the technological breakthroughs, geopolitical maneuvering, economic pressures, and existential risks inherent in this transformative race. Your goal is to achieve global dominance, measured primarily by **Victory Points (VP)**, secure a decisive AGI lead, or navigate the escalating global instability better than your opponent. But beware – pushing too hard or too recklessly could trigger global crises or even catastrophic outcomes.

## Core Concepts

* **Influence:** Represents your superpower's sway in key global centers relevant to AI. You place Influence markers in **Hubs** on the game map.
* **Hubs:** Abstract locations representing research labs, policy centers, compute clusters, public opinion spheres, or military commands, grouped into five strategic **Domains**.
* **Domains:** The main arenas of competition: **R&D**, **Compute & Hardware**, **Policy & Governance**, **Public Opinion**, and **Military Integration**. Control within these Domains is key to scoring VP.
* **AGI Development Track:** A measure of each nation's progress towards AGI/ASI. Advancing yields benefits but carries increasing risks.
* **Global Stability Track:** Represents world stability. Starts high (5) and decreases due to aggressive actions, crises, or AGI breakthroughs. Low stability makes actions harder and triggers **Crisis Cards**. Reaching 0 Stability results in game loss for the player responsible.
* **Cards:** The heart of the game. Each card has:
  * **Operations Points (Ops):** Used to take actions. Comes in three types: Research (🔴), Compute (🟣), and Influence (🔵).
  * **Event:** A powerful, specific historical or thematic effect.
  * **Affiliation:** USA, China, or Neutral.
* **Crisis Cards:** Drawn when Stability drops low or the AGI track hits milestones. Represent negative events that disrupt play and must often be dealt with.

## The Map

The game features an abstract "Nexus" map:
* A central area displays the **VP Track** and **Global Stability Track**.
* Five distinct **Domain Sectors** surround the Nexus, each containing relevant **Hubs**.
* Lines between Hubs indicate **Connections**, necessary for spreading Influence.
* Separate **AGI Tracks** for USA and China show technological progress.

## Sequence of Play (Turn Overview)

The game is played over approximately 10 turns. Each turn follows this general sequence:

1. **Stability Phase:** Check Global Stability. Improve Stability slightly if below maximum. Low Stability may trigger Crisis Card draws. Apply ongoing Crisis effects.
2. **Card Phase:** Players draw cards up to their hand size for the current era.
3. **Headline Phase:** Players secretly select one card. Cards are revealed simultaneously, and their Events resolve in order determined by Compute Ops value (higher first, USA breaks ties).
4. **Action Rounds:** Players alternate playing cards (typically 6-7 rounds per turn). China takes the first Action Round.
5. **Crisis Resolution Phase:** Address mandatory effects or choices presented by active Crisis Cards.
6. **Cleanup Phase:** Discard down to hand limit (usually 1 card). Reset temporary effects. Check Victory Conditions. Advance Turn Marker.

## Playing Cards: The Core Choice

During each Action Round, the active player MUST play one card from their hand in one of two ways:

1. **For Operations Points (Ops):**
   * Use the Ops value (🔴, 🟣, 🔵) printed on the card to take actions (see below).
   * **CRITICAL RULE:** If the card played has the **opponent's** affiliation (e.g., USA plays a China card), the **Event on that card MUST trigger for the opponent**. The player playing the card chooses whether the event happens *before* or *after* they spend the Ops points.
2. **For the Event:**
   * Trigger the card's Event text immediately. *You can only play your own or Neutral cards this way.*
   * The Ops value of the card is ignored. The card is usually discarded (or removed from the game if specified).

## Spending Ops Points (Core Actions)

When you play a card for Ops, you use those points to perform actions, typically focusing on one main action type per card play:

* **Place Influence (🔵):** Spend Influence Ops to add your markers to Hubs on the map. You generally need a connection from an adjacent Hub you already influence. Placing influence in uncontrolled or friendly hubs is cheaper (usually 1 Op per point) than in enemy-controlled hubs (usually 2 Ops per point). Costs can be modified by events or stability.
* **Advance AGI Track (🔴/🟣):** Spend Research and Compute Ops to move your marker forward on your AGI Track. Costs increase significantly at higher levels. Reaching milestones triggers effects (new cards, Stability checks, VP, potential Crises).
* **Mitigate Stability/Crisis (🔴/🟣/🔵):** Spend specific Ops types (as dictated by the Crisis card or game situation) to increase Global Stability or resolve/mitigate the effects of an active Crisis card.

## Scoring

* Victory Points (VP) are tracked on the central VP track (-20 USA win to +20 China win).
* Scoring occurs *only* when a player plays a **Domain Scoring Card** during an Action Round.
* Players compare their level of control within that Domain's Hubs:
  * **Presence:** Control at least 1 Hub. (Worth 1 VP)
  * **Domination:** Control more Hubs than the opponent. (Worth 3 VP)
  * **Control:** Control more Hubs AND all high-stability Hubs in the Domain. (Worth 6 VP)
* Bonus VP may be awarded based on specific conditions listed on the Scoring card.
* The net difference in VP gained by both players adjusts the marker on the VP Track.

## Winning the Game

You can win *AGI Race: Brinkmanship* in several ways:

1. **VP Victory:** Reach 20 VP on your side of the track immediately.
2. **Stability Collapse:** Your opponent performs an action that causes Global Stability to reach 0.
3. **AGI Takeoff (Late Game):** Achieve a decisive lead on the AGI Track under specific conditions (often linked to high Stability and/or card play).
4. **End of Game:** If no one has won by the end of the final turn, a final scoring round occurs for all Domains. The player with the VP advantage wins (tiebreakers may apply).

*Get ready to navigate the complex interplay of technological progress, geopolitical tension, and global risk management. Good luck, Commander!*
